Changes that matter

How to read the map

A primary category gives each entry one position. Capability tags show overlap; the detailed recommended placement preserves the researcher's more specific classification. Original categories and links are retained for comparison.

Technology supplies products or platforms. Enabling technology covers general integration, product data, or supporting hardware. Services covers advisory, implementation, and operational delivery. Some businesses span these types; the placement is editorial, not a corporate classification.

National medicines verification infrastructure is added as a separate category. Its commercial technology vendors are distinct from regulators, standards organizations, and system operators.

Evidence rules

Supported Primary-source product, service, technical, or customer evidence supports the stated offering. Vendor claims are attributed evidence, not independent validation.

Partial evidence Identity, current availability, specialized functionality, or category fit remains unresolved; this also includes generic infrastructure without a demonstrated specialized pharmaceutical offering.

Unverified The reviewed evidence did not establish the stated capability. This does not mean the provider lacks it.

A published page can be old. Access during this review does not establish its publication date or prove that a product is still sold. Known limitations are retained in the record.

Standards and ecosystem context

The FDA describes DSCSA as package-level electronic identification and tracing. Its product-tracing FAQ recommends GS1 EPCIS for enhanced electronic data exchange. EPCIS provides a common structure for traceability events; it is distinct from EDI transport, identity credentials, product authentication, and verification routing.

FDA: DSCSA · FDA: product-tracing FAQ · GS1: EPCIS · EMVO: gateway directory

GS1, FDA, EMVO and national system operators belong in the ecosystem context, not the commercial provider count. Directory inclusion alone does not establish a vendor’s full current functionality or certification status.

Classification rules

Each entry has one primary placement for navigation. Capabilities overlap; placement does not establish exclusivity or a tested integration. Named products require a distinct function and a sourced provider relationship. Records are not independent-company counts.

Remaining diligence

Prioritize the review queue, then validate country-specific reporting interfaces, deployed product versions, parent-company relationships, and commercial availability with providers. Compare two-way exchange and exception workflows using actual technical documentation before treating products as interoperable.
